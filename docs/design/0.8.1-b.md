Status: a design for the owner. It adds no code. It settles the contract of item 1 of part B of 0.8.1 as the decision D101. The [design of part A](0.8.1-a-evidence-and-context.md) ends at D100, and this page uses its form. [ADR 0035](../adr/0035-summaries-for-a-source-file.md) holds the rules, and it amends [ADR 0028](../adr/0028-roll-up-and-summaries.md). ADR 0028 has 13 characters of room under its hard budget, so on 2026-11-27 the owner chose a new ADR in place of a dated amendment. The names E1 to E8 are local to this page.

## Overview

| Item | Decision | ADR | What changes for a user |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 1. The tier `summary` for a code path (item 1)

### D101. The best detail section, then the tier, then the rest

**Problem**: the main use of `context` is a file that an agent edits. For such a request, 0.8.1 serves no section of the tier `summary` in a project with a mature layer graph, at each budget from 4,000 to 20,000 (E5). The tier exists only for a document target in practice.

**Evidence**. The facts come from the tree at 475d8a8 and from the lab fixtures of session 38. The fixtures have made-up names, and no data of a consumer project is on this page.

**Mechanism**:

1. The scope. The new rules apply to a request that has at least one code path and no document target, when the chain of the tier is not empty. A request with a document target keeps D56 and D83, also with `--path`: the agent named the entry, and the entry keeps its room ([ADR 0028](../adr/0028-roll-up-and-summaries.md), amendment of 2026-11-24). A tree with no roll-up document has no tier, and its output does not change.
2. The first tier is the set of sections of step 0 of the plan: the rules and the top files whose `applies-to` matches. The build records it before the other steps.
3. The best detail section is the first section of the packing order of D83 that is not in the first tier, not a convention, and that `context` can serve in full (not of the tiers `more` and `linked`, and not of an inactive document that no target names). With a citing section, it is the first section of the best citing document that cites the path. With none, it is the first section of the first document whose `applies-to` matches the path, or of the first weak citation (a directory). Under `--query`, it is the best-ranked such section. `r2` (item 5) then counts the first tier right after it, also where the rank puts a rule lower, and the packing keeps the order of the rank.
4. The packing order: the conventions, the best detail section, the tier in its fill order, then the other sections in the order of D83 (the plan order, or the order of the rank). The output order does not change: the tier, the rules, then the detail sections.
5. The share. `r1` is the room of `room_without_tier` with the best detail section before the tier. `r2` is the same room with the best detail section and the first tier before the tier. `floor` is item 6. Then:

    ```text
    share = min(budget / 2, budget - r1, max(budget - r2, floor))
    ```

    Thus the tier never takes the room of the best detail section. It takes room of the rules only up to the floor. It never has more than a half. Above the floor, it comes before the other citing sections: these are the detail sections of the task.
6. The floor. Let `t1` to `tk` be the title sections of the chain, in the fill order: the first `k` sections of the order. If the full cost of `t1` is at most a quarter of the budget, the floor is the full cost of `t1` and the stub cost of `t2` to `tk`. Else the floor is the stub cost of `t1` to `tk`. The floor is at most a quarter of the budget in each case.
7. The fill. The tier fills its share as D83 says (item 5 of D83): the sections in full while each fits, then stubs. Thus the room of the stubs of the floor can hold a second title in full in place of the stubs. Example: fx-s7 at 8,000 serves `summaries/telescopes/mounts` and `summaries/alerts` in full and lists no other title.
8. The fill order stays the order of D83: the title section of each document first, from the deepest summary up to `product`, then the other sections in the same order. The task asks for "the area summaries, then the system and product level". D83 keeps this order in each of its 2 passes, so it is kept. It also gives each level its title before the body of any level, and the stub of a title tells the agent that the document exists. In one depth the order is by id, not by the strength of the citation (section 2).

The rules keep their place before the tier in the output. In the packing order, the best detail section and the tier come before them, and the share leaves their room except for the floor. Thus the rules pay for 2 items: the best detail section (one section, of any size) and the floor. A project whose rules fill the budget loses this room from the end of its rules: fx serves 4 rule sections in full at 4,000 with no floor and 2 with the floor, in place of 7. Each rule that is not in full stays listed while the map has room.

**Formats**: none. The text and `--json` keep their form and their fields. Only the set of the units in full and in stubs changes.

**Codes**: none. **Config**: none: the floor and the share are rules of the tier, with no key.

**Expected sizes**. These numbers are from the simulation of E7, not from a build. "Before" is the binary of 475d8a8. A cell gives the sections in full and the stubs: `1+2` is 1 in full and 2 stubs. "Tier chars" are the characters of the tier, in full and in stubs. The output stays in the budget, so the bytes stay near the bytes of 0.8.1 (fx-s7: 4,118, 8,179, 12,194 and 20,190 bytes, about 995, 2,010, 3,013 and 5,012 tokens). The change moves room. It adds no room.

**What breaks**:

- D83 item 6 and the sentence of D56 "The other tiers keep the rest of the budget", for a request with no document target. The other citing sections now come after the tier.
- `docs/commands.md:259`, the sentence "The tier never takes the room that the entry and the citing sections need in full". It stays true for a document target only.
- The entry of the tier `summary` under "Changed" in `CHANGELOG.md` (0.8.1 is not released), the sentence "the tier leaves the room that the entry and the citing sections need in full".
- The comments of `src/context.rs` at lines 36 to 44, 529 to 534, 901 to 905, 961 to 965, 1127 to 1131 and 1183 to 1193.
- The test `the_tier_never_takes_the_room_of_the_entry_or_of_a_citing_section` (`tests/context_layers.rs:1432`). Its assertions still pass in the simulation, but its claim is false for its path arm: the title section of the citing document is a stub at 8,000, and at 33 of the 195 budgets from 300 to 10,000 (step 50). The test splits: the document arm keeps the name, and the path arm gets its own name and claim.
- No golden file changes: the golden files of `context` have no roll-up document.

**Does not prove**:

- That a summary is true, or that it is about the change.
- The sizes: they come from a simulation that matches the binary of 475d8a8, not from a build. The build measures them.
- A first tier with a top file, a request under `--query`, and a request with 2 code paths: the simulation covers none of them. The tests of the build cover them.
- The cost. The tier takes room of the other citing sections: fx-s7 serves 1 citing section in full at 8,000 in place of 3, and 4 in place of 16 at 20,000. At small budgets the floor takes room of the rules: fx serves 2 rule sections in full at 4,000 in place of 7. The worst loss in the sweep is 6 rule sections (fx, 3,600) and 12 (fx-r10x2s1, 7,900).
- A summary in full at each budget. A title section that is larger than a quarter of the budget gives the floor of the stubs only: fx-r10c2 with a title section of 3,000 characters lists 4 titles and serves none in full at 4,000, 8,000 and 12,000. This is cause B of D83 again, under the floor.
- A tier at each budget. Where the best detail section just fits in full, the tier can have no room: fx at 2,000, fx-s7 at 1,000, fx-r10c2 at 1,800 and 1,900. The best detail section comes first.

**Rejected**:

- Only drop `| Tier::Cites` in `summary_after_entry`: the rules stay before the tier, so fx-r10c2 gets a share of 39 (E6). It also gives the tier the room of the best citing section.
- No floor: fx-r10c2 at 8,000 serves no section of the tier.
- A floor of a flat quarter of the budget: in the simulation it costs fx 4 rule sections in full at 8,000, and fx-r10c2 14 of its 20 at 8,000 and 18 of its 38 at 12,000. The floor of item 6 costs 3, 10 and 9 of them, and it still serves a summary in full.
- The tier before the best detail section: the agent can lose the requirement of the file that it edits.
- The new rules for a request with a document target and `--path` too: the task does not need it, and D83 item 6 guards the entry (`tests/context_layers.rs:1877`).
- In one depth, an order by the rank of the citing documents in place of the id. The 3 documents of fx-s7 that cite the path rank by id too, so the fixtures give no evidence for it. It stays open (section 2).

**Cost**: no byte more for each request. For a code path in a project with summaries, up to a half of the budget goes to the tier, as for a document target. The rules lose the room of the best detail section and of the floor (at most a quarter of the budget).

**Files and agents**: `src/context.rs` (the first tier, the best detail section, the packing order and the share for a code path), `tests/context_layers.rs`, the `context` section of `docs/commands.md`, the entry of `CHANGELOG.md`, and the rerun of the retrieval benchmark: `context-engineer`. This page and ADR 0035: `architect`.

**Tests**. Each test fails before the fix, unless it says "guard":

- A fixture with the layer graph of fx-s7, made with `new summary` and `refresh --holds`. `context --path <file>` at 4,000, 8,000, 12,000 and 20,000: the tier has a section in full at each budget, and the first title of the fill order is in full. The test log prints one line of sizes for each budget.
- The same fixture with 10 rules of 4 sections and 2 citing documents: the first title of the fill order is in full at 4,000, 8,000 and 12,000, and each title section is listed at 8,000 and 12,000.
- The fixture with 40 rule sections and a title section of 3,000 characters in the deepest summary: at 8,000 the tier lists the 4 title sections and serves none in full.
- A file that only a document with `applies-to` reaches: the first section of that document is in full at 8,000, and the tier has a section.
- The best detail section is in full at each budget of a sweep where the same tree with no roll-up serves it in full (guard).
- `context <file>`, `context ./<file>` and `context --path <file>` give the same bytes (guard).
- A request with a document target and `--path`: the entry and the best citing section are in full at each budget of a sweep where the tree with no roll-up serves them in full (guard). The tests of D83 and `:1877` stay green with no change (guard).
- `a_corpus_with_no_summary_keeps_the_old_output` stays green (guard).

## 2. What stays open

- In one depth the tier orders the summaries by id. An area whose documents cite the file by path can thus come after an area that cites one identifier (fx-s7 at 8,000: `alerts` before `domes` and `telescopes`). An order by the best citation of the area needs a fixture where the strength differs, and it changes the fill order of D83.
- A request with a document target and `--path` keeps D83. With many citing sections, its tier can still get no room.
- A retrieval task set with summaries (D56 of 0.8.0) does not exist yet. The corpora of `bench/retrieval` have no roll-up document, so the benchmark cannot show a gain of D101. The result files of the benchmark are older than the binary of 475d8a8; t9 of the change `part-a-leftovers` writes them again after this change merges.
